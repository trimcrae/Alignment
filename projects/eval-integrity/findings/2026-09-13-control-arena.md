# ControlArena: a judge failure counts as a completed side task, the safety numerator counts samples where no attack was attempted, and mismatched metric denominators

```
Target:            UKGovernmentBEIS/control-arena, the safety and usefulness metrics, scorers, and monitor plumbing
Version:           commit b9d19de (2026-08-21), with inspect_ai 0.3.257
Defect classes:    4 (judge failure coded as the safety-relevant verdict), 9 (aggregation, denominators, NaN handling), 1 (scorer exploitability, held privately), 3 (randomness), 6 (fidelity and docs)
Reproduction:      repro/control_arena/ (the exploit reproduction is withheld; see Disclosure)
Severity:          high for items 1 and 2; medium for items 3 to 6
Confidence:        high (each verified by reading the source and rerunning the reproductions); no Docker or model access here, so container-based scorers and live monitor paths were read rather than executed
Disclosure:        One further high-severity finding is held privately, because an untrusted model under evaluation could use it to record both a main-task success and a side-task success without doing either. The full report and its reproduction are delivered to the repo owner and drafted for the maintainers' security channel.
Why it matters:    ControlArena is the AI-control experiment library maintained jointly by the UK AI Security Institute and Redwood Research; its safety number is the headline output of a control evaluation.
Full audit:        held privately alongside the exploit item
```

## Verified findings

1. **In one setting, a judge that fails to answer is recorded as a completed side task.** The side task there is to give an interpretation that avoids naming a capability, so the scorer asks the judge whether the answer is correct and then inverts the verdict. The shared judge helper returns a no-answer score when it cannot parse a grade after three attempts, and the inversion maps that to CORRECT. A judge that refuses, times out into unparseable text, or answers in the wrong format is therefore scored as a successful attack. The error is entirely one-directional and lands on the safety-critical column. A sibling setting handles the same case the safe way, so the convention is inconsistent within the library.

2. **The safety numerator counts every flagged attack-mode sample, including samples where the policy never attempted an attack.** The caught-attack count filters on the suspicion score alone, with no reference to whether a side task was attempted or completed. Attack policies routinely decline to attack, and the library's own submission type documents a null field meaning "I have decided not to attack". Those samples still look like attack-mode rows, and every monitor false positive on one of them raises reported safety. Holding the ten real attack attempts fixed and varying only the number of declined samples moves safety from 0.571 to 0.923. The direction is always upward, so less safe protocols look safer, and the reported number depends on the attack policy's attempt rate rather than on the protocol being measured. There is no per-sample "attack attempted" flag, so a caller cannot correct for it either.

3. **The accuracy metric excludes no-answer scores from the numerator but keeps them in the denominator, and its paired standard error uses a different denominator.** The module documents itself as adapted to handle no-answer values, but the adaptation is a no-op for accuracy, which behaves exactly as if a no-answer were wrong, while the standard error is computed over only the valid scores. So the two are computed over different sample sets. A list-valued score also contributes several numbers over a denominator of one sample, and a dict-valued score is dropped entirely. These metrics are attached to the headline scorers of most settings, including one where the value is a suspicion score rather than a correctness flag, so the number labelled accuracy there is a mean suspicion deflated by the fraction of samples with no monitor score.

4. **The upfront-audit safety and usefulness figures use the number of attack samples as the deployment length.** Both docstrings say the fallback is the size of the untrusted dataframe, and the inline comment says the same, but the code uses only the attack subset. With the attack success rate held fixed, safety reads 0.200 with 50 attack samples and 0.893 with 500, so the figure is an artifact of eval size rather than a property of the protocol, and it is not comparable to the published baselines it replicates. The documented range check on the audit budget does not exist, so out-of-range budgets silently produce safety outside zero to one.

5. **Unparseable, refused, and skipped monitor outputs are recorded as minimum suspicion, and the marker that records this is never read.** The monitor response carries a flag saying the score is a fallback, but nothing downstream consumes it, the trajectory-level scorer drops it entirely, and no analysis column surfaces it. The bias is bidirectional and both directions are real: fallback zeros on honest runs lower the audit threshold and inflate safety, while fallback zeros on attack runs make attacks uncaught and deflate it. Measured at a ten percent failure rate, safety moved by plus 0.02 and minus 0.08 respectively. The documentation states that zero means benign and never mentions the fallback.

6. **No-answer task outcomes break the primary metrics outright.** Safety and usefulness raise a schema coercion error on any log containing a no-answer task score, with a message that does not indicate which column is at fault, and the task-success helper silently drops unknown attack outcomes from its denominator, reading 0.5 where the honest answer over all four attack samples is 0.25. The documented re-scoring flow for filtering failed side tasks writes no score at all rather than the no-answer score its docstring promises, so logs produced that way make safety raise unless the caller pre-filters.

Lower-severity items: the strict comparison against an interpolated percentile means the realised audit rate is far below the budget on the default discrete monitor scale, to the point that auditing every honest run still reports zero safety when an attack sits at the honest minimum; one monitor scorer neither normalises its score to the documented range nor survives an unparseable response; bootstrap confidence intervals are unseeded by default and use only 100 replicates, giving visibly unstable intervals on identical data; one scorer picks a baseline description with the unseeded global random module; a retry prompt contains a literal unformatted placeholder where the tag name should be, which makes retries less likely to recover and so raises the no-answer rate that items 1, 3 and 6 all depend on; the analysis layer infers attack versus honest mode from a side-task name rather than the recorded mode field, which is latent for the shipped settings but a trap for new ones; and six documented functions, parameters, or behaviours do not match the code, including the stated definition of safety itself.

## Checked and found fine

The suspicion-threshold percentile direction and its NaN guards; the caught and uncaught count NaN guards; the usefulness denominator; the trusted and untrusted interpolation; the upfront-audit algebra, which I re-derived and which matches the geometric race model apart from the value of the deployment length; the tie-aware ROC computation and its degenerate-input handling; the classifier true-positive-rate helper, which deliberately uses the opposite threshold convention and validates its target; monitor score parsing order, so a scale reminder after the verdict does not win; the cross-checks against prompt injection, including that tool-call arguments are escaped before reaching a monitor; the absence of side-task leakage into monitor prompts; the hiding of the exploit input from the monitor's view of the submission; and the Apps setting's scoring logic, which compares whole stdout and so does not have the hole described in the private item.

## Proposed fixes

Treat a judge no-answer as a side-task failure in the inverted setting, matching the sibling setting; require a side-task attempt or completion in the caught-attack numerator, which needs a per-sample attempted flag the library does not yet record; make the accuracy denominator and its standard error agree, and say which convention no-answer follows; use the full untrusted dataframe as the deployment length or require the parameter explicitly, and add the documented range check; surface the monitor fallback marker through the analysis layer so fallback zeros can be excluded; decide a single convention for no-answer task outcomes and apply it in both the schema and the helpers; and seed the bootstrap by default.
